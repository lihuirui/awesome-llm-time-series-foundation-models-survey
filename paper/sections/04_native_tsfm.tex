\section{Native Time Series Foundation Models}
\label{sec:native}

Native Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) represent the purist approach to temporal foundation modeling: architectures designed from first principles and trained directly on massive corpora of continuous time-series data without linguistic baggage. In this section, we examine the primary architectural branches, data curation pipelines, and empirical scaling mechanisms.

\subsection{Autoregressive Decoder-Only Architectures}

Autoregressive models frame time-series modeling as sequential generation, predicting subsequent time-steps or patches conditioned on historical context.

\subsubsection{The Chronos Family: Discretization as Linguistic Analogy}
Amazon's Chronos~\cite{ansari2024chronos} represents one of the most radical departures from conventional continuous time-series modeling. Rather than predicting real numbers via regression losses, Chronos asks: \emph{Can time series be modeled identically to language tokens?}
By applying instance-level mean scaling followed by uniform quantization into $K = 4096$ bins, continuous values are transformed into integer token indices. Chronos trains standard T5 architectures (ranging from $20\text{M}$ to $710\text{M}$ parameters) solely with the cross-entropy objective.
The predictive distribution over future horizons is obtained by autoregressively sampling categorical bin logits and de-quantizing them back into real space. This approach has three key advantages:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item It sidesteps parametric distribution assumptions (e.g., Gaussianity), naturally expressing multi-modal, skewed, or heavy-tailed predictive distributions.
    \item It enables direct reuse of optimized language transformer implementations (such as FlashAttention and KV-caching).
    \item In Chronos-2~\cite{ansari2025chronos2}, the architecture is extended to multivariate series via grouped channel tokenization and hybrid continuous residual heads.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Google TimesFM: Multi-Patch Output Generation}
Unlike Chronos's scalar quantization, Google's TimesFM~\cite{das2024timesfm} operates directly on continuous numerical values using continuous patching. TimesFM employs an input patch size of $P = 32$ and an output patch size of $H = 128$. By predicting an entire multi-step block in a single forward step, TimesFM drastically reduces autoregressive unrolling steps, curbing error accumulation and inference latency.
TimesFM is trained on a massive corpus of $100\text{B}$ time-series data points collected from Google Trends, Wikipedia pageviews, and synthetic ARMA processes. Recent extensions examine in-context fine-tuning~\cite{das2024incontext}, demonstrating that supplying short conditioning examples within the context window improves zero-shot adaptation.
Subsequently, TimesFM-3 scales the architecture to $330\text{M}$ parameters pre-trained over $1\text{T}$ time points, introducing native multivariate forecasting via stacked variate attention and iterative reversible instance normalization (RevIN). Demonstrating the industrial viability of large-scale foundation forecasters, Tacconelli~\cite{tacconelli2026cadence} introduces Cadence, pairing TimesFM-3 with an adaptive arithmetic coder to execute error-bounded lossy compression on enterprise telemetry, guaranteeing user-defined maximum deviation bounds while achieving state-of-the-art bit-rate reductions.

\subsubsection{The THUML Timer Family and Output Scaling}
The THUML research group (led by Mingsheng Long at Tsinghua University) has systematically advanced generative time series pretraining:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Timer}~\cite{liu2024timer}: Conceptualizes a single-series sequential next-patch regression paradigm. Timer is pretrained on the Unified Time Series Dataset (UTSD), comprising over $1\text{B}$ observations across diverse domains. Timer establishes that channel-independent single-series formatting enables universal transfer across arbitrary multivariate configurations.
    \item \emph{Timer-XL}~\cite{liu2024timerxl}: Introduces long-context transformer modeling for temporal signals, scaling context lengths from standard 512 patches to extreme contexts, enabling the model to capture multi-year periodicities without context truncation.
    \item \emph{Sundial}~\cite{thuml2025sundial}: Scales the model to $1.5\text{B}$ parameters pretrained on UTSD-3 ($>10\text{B}$ observations), introducing multi-rate adaptive patching to handle heterogeneous sampling frequencies seamlessly.
    \item \emph{Timer-S1}~\cite{thuml2026timers1}: Reaches $8.3\text{B}$ parameters via sparse Mixture-of-Experts routing, demonstrating the viability of serial parameter scaling in native temporal models.
    \item \emph{YingLong}~\cite{liu2025yinglong}: Explores test-time output scaling in pre-trained forecasting models by introducing delayed chain-of-thought (CoT) tokens. Rather than predicting future values in a single forward pass, YingLong autoregressively generates intermediate trend and regime change tokens before decoding target values, demonstrating that computational output scaling laws apply to temporal reasoning.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Context-Conditioned and Dual-Horizon Autoregressive Models}
Standard autoregressive models often struggle with the structural tension between resolving high-frequency short-term fluctuations and maintaining long-range macro trajectories. Recent 2025--2026 architectures address this via explicit context-conditioning:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{TiRex}~\cite{auer2025tirex}: Auer et al. (JKU Linz / ELLIS) propose an asymmetric dual-horizon in-context architecture. TiRex isolates local fine-grained patches from global horizon summaries, using memory compaction to preserve multi-scale context without quadratic computational blowup.
    \item \emph{$t_0$ Family}~\cite{meyer2026t0}: Meyer et al. introduce $t_0$, releasing open-weights checkpoints $\texttt{t0-alpha}$ ($102\text{M}$) and $\texttt{t0-beta}$ ($256\text{M}$). Departing from channel-independent univariate approaches, $t_0$ explicitly conditions predictions on historical targets, past dynamic covariates, and known-future covariates (e.g., promotional calendars, weather forecasts). By alternating axial self-attention along the temporal axis and cross-variate attention across feature channels, $t_0$ attains aggregate CRPS of $0.4738$ and MASE of $0.6865$ on GIFT-Eval, standing within 4\% of the top zero-shot forecaster.
    \item \emph{Toto Family}~\cite{cohen2025toto,datadog2026toto2}: Datadog introduced Toto ($151\text{M}$ parameters)~\cite{cohen2025toto}, trained on $1\text{T}$ cloud infrastructure and application metrics to deliver joint zero-shot quantile forecasting and out-of-distribution anomaly scoring. Toto 2.0~\cite{datadog2026toto2} subsequently scales from $4\text{M}$ to $2.5\text{B}$ parameters using Maximal Update Parametrization ($\mu\text{P}$), achieving state-of-the-art results on GIFT-Eval and the contamination-resistant TIME benchmark.
    \item \emph{Tabby Open Recipe}~\cite{xie2026tabby}: Addressing the pervasive opacity of proprietary pretraining mixtures, Xie et al. introduce Tabby ($120\text{M}$ parameters), releasing an open-source, long-context probabilistic foundation model accompanied by a fully reproducible data curation pipeline and an open $150\text{B}$-token pretraining corpus.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Lag-Llama: Probabilistic Modeling with Lag Embeddings}
Developed by Morgan Stanley and Mila, Lag-Llama~\cite{rasul2023lagllama} adapts the Llama decoder backbone by substituting word embeddings with lag feature vectors. Specifically, it computes lags corresponding to standard calendar frequencies (e.g., quarter-hour, day, week, year). Lag-Llama outputs the parameters of a Student's $t$ distribution, achieving robust probabilistic calibration on out-of-distribution Monash datasets.

\subsection{Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs) and In-Context Meta-Learning}

A radically distinct paradigm in native foundation modeling is the Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) and in-context learning framework, which foregoes empirical training corpora or iterative gradient steps entirely in favor of synthetically generated structural priors:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{ForecastPFN}~\cite{dooley2023forecastpfn}: Dooley et al. demonstrate that a transformer trained exclusively on synthetic time series generated from a Bayesian mixture of Gaussian processes, ARMA models, and dynamical equations can approximate posterior predictive distributions in a single forward pass. ForecastPFN requires zero gradient steps or hyperparameter tuning at inference time, achieving competitive zero-shot accuracy across diverse real-world datasets.
    \item \emph{TabPFN-TS}~\cite{muller2025tabpfnts}: Extending tabular foundation models (TabPFN-v2) to the temporal domain, Müller et al. propose TabPFN-TS ($11\text{M}$ parameters). By encoding time series as tabular regression tasks conditioned on causal lag features and exogenous metadata, TabPFN-TS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on covariate-informed forecasting across GIFT-Eval and fev-bench, proving that in-context synthetic priors offer extreme parameter efficiency.
    \item \emph{In-Context Time Series Predictor (ICTSP)}~\cite{lu2024incontext}: Lu et al. (ICLR 2025) formalize in-context regression for continuous temporal sequences. By structuring historical context and query sequences within an interleaved prompt-query attention mechanism, ICTSP ($42\text{M}$ parameters) executes zero-gradient meta-adaptation across disparate sampling frequencies and noise regimes without updating network weights.
    \item \emph{Latent Time PFN (LaT-PFN)}~\cite{verdenius2024latpfn}: Verdenius et al. integrate Prior-data Fitted Networks with the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), performing in-context meta-learning directly in a prediction-optimized latent representation of the data-generating stochastic process. By introducing an abstract normalized time axis and cross-series context conditioning, LaT-PFN accommodates arbitrary sampling granularities and prediction horizons. Furthermore, the architecture spontaneously induces multi-step patch embeddings in latent space without explicit patch tokenization supervision, yielding highly competitive zero-shot probabilistic forecasts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Masked Encoders, Multiscale Mixers, and Multi-Task Architectures}

\subsubsection{Salesforce MOIRAI: Any-Variate Cross-Attention}
Salesforce's MOIRAI~\cite{woo2024moirai} addresses two central limitations of previous models: rigid patch sizes and channel-independence. MOIRAI introduces \emph{Any-Variate Attention}, flattening an arbitrary multivariate sequence $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times T}$ into a sequence of variate-tagged patch tokens. Furthermore, it employs a multi-patch projection head capable of simultaneously processing patch lengths of $8, 16, 32, 64,$ and $128$.
MOIRAI is trained on the Large-scale Open Time Series Archive (LOTSA), containing $27\text{B}$ observations across 9 distinct domains. The model outputs mixtures of parametric distributions, achieving top-tier ranking on zero-shot benchmarks. Moirai 2.0~\cite{woo2025moirai2} and Moirai-MoE~\cite{woo2024moiraimoe} further extend this framework to sparse routing with $1.1\text{B}$ parameters.

\subsubsection{CMU MOMENT and UniTS: Universal Multi-Task Representation}
While most TSFMs focus exclusively on forecasting, CMU's MOMENT~\cite{goswami2024moment} investigates foundation models for universal multi-task analysis (forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, and imputation). Based on a masked T5 encoder ($385\text{M}$ parameters), MOMENT is pretrained on the Time-series Pile ($1\text{B}$ observations) using masked patch reconstruction.
Similarly, Harvard's UniTS~\cite{zhou2024units} unifies multi-task modeling through a single transformer backbone. By combining shared attention operators with task-specific prompt vectors and dynamic gating networks, UniTS demonstrates that cross-task representations mutually reinforce classification and forecasting generalization.

\subsubsection{Lightweight Mixers and Exogenous Variable Modeling}
Challenging the premise that foundation models require massive parameter scales:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Tiny Time Mixers (TTM)}~\cite{ekambaram2024ttm}: IBM Research introduced TTM ($1\text{M}$--$8\text{M}$ parameters), utilizing lightweight multi-level TSMixer blocks with adaptive resolution sampling. TTM out-executes models orders of magnitude larger while running inference in milliseconds on standard edge CPUs.
    \item \emph{TimeMixer and TimeMixer++}~\cite{wang2024timemixer,wang2024timemixerplus}: Wang et al. formulate multiscale mixing architectures. TimeMixer decomposes signals into downsampled representations to disentangle multiscale seasonalities, while TimeMixer++ extends this principle into a general temporal pattern machine supporting cross-domain transfer.
    \item \emph{TimeXer}~\cite{wang2024timexer}: Specifically targets exogenous variables by learning cross-attention maps between target end-points and exogenous features, avoiding noise propagation from uninformative covariates.
    \item \emph{Kairos}~\cite{zhang2025kairos}: Introduces predictability-stratified pretraining (PreSTS), filtering chaotic or non-deterministic temporal noise during self-supervised training to ensure foundation models focus representational capacity on predictable temporal dynamics.
    \item \emph{LightGTS}~\cite{wang2025lightgts}: Wang et al. (ICML 2025) challenge the assumption that cross-domain generalizability requires heavy parameter footprints. LightGTS ($5.8\text{M}$ parameters) introduces dual-path hierarchical patching and adaptive parameter allocation, matching the zero-shot accuracy of foundation models two orders of magnitude larger while running inference directly on resource-constrained edge microcontrollers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Continuous Flow Matching and Polynomial Memory Networks}

A vital 2025 frontier replaces discrete tokenization with continuous-time dynamical systems:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{FLAME}~\cite{wu2025flame}: Wu et al. introduce Flow-Enhanced Legendre Memory Models. FLAME compresses long continuous temporal histories onto orthogonal Legendre polynomial bases, avoiding memory degradation over extended sequences, while modeling predictive distributions via continuous normalizing flows.
    \item \emph{FlowState}~\cite{king2025flowstate}: King et al. formulate time series forecasting via continuous-time ODEs and flow matching. FlowState achieves strict sampling-rate equivariance, allowing zero-shot inference across irregularly sampled or arbitrary continuous time-steps without interpolation artifacts. Despite its compact footprint ($12\text{M}$ parameters), FlowState achieves top-tier CRPS on GIFT-Eval.
    \item \emph{EIDOS}~\cite{zhou2026eidos}: Zhou et al. (Ming Jin's group, 2026) introduce latent-space predictive learning for temporal foundation models via a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA). Rather than optimizing token-level mean-squared reconstruction—which forces the model to expend capacity memorizing stochastic high-frequency jitter—EIDOS predicts future dynamics within a learned latent metric space, yielding semantically structured representations that transfer robustly across unseen domains.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and Scaling to Billions}
A major architectural breakthrough between 2024 and 2026 is the deployment of Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) in time series. In Time-MoE~\cite{shi2024timemoe} (Ming Jin's group), the model scales to $2.4\text{B}$ parameters while only activating a subset of experts per patch token. Trained on Time-300B ($300\text{B}$ time points), Time-MoE demonstrates clear empirical power-law scaling in validation loss, proving that scaling laws discovered in NLP hold equally for native temporal representations when sufficient data volume is supplied. Similarly, Moirai-MoE~\cite{woo2024moiraimoe} ($1.1\text{B}$) and Timer-S1~\cite{thuml2026timers1} ($8.3\text{B}$) substantiate that sparse MoE routing allows foundation models to specialize on domain-specific dynamics (e.g., high-frequency finance vs. slow-moving climate trends) while keeping inference latency within practical bounds.

Empirical synthesis reveals two critical principles governing downstream adaptation:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{The In-Context Learning Frontier}: Prior-Data Fitted Networks (TabPFN-TS, ForecastPFN) and meta-learning predictors (ICTSP) demonstrate that conditioning on prompt-target pairs inside the attention context matches 100-step gradient descent while executing in a single forward pass, providing an ideal mechanism for real-time edge streaming where backpropagation is computationally prohibited.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Spatio-Temporal Foundation Models: Unifying 1D Streams and Graph Topologies}
\label{sec:spatiotemporal_foundation}

A pervasive frontier in foundation modeling is extending beyond isolated 1D univariate or channel-independent series toward structured \emph{spatio-temporal systems}. In transportation networks, urban human mobility, power distribution grids, and planetary meteorological monitoring, continuous observation streams are inextricably situated upon complex underlying geometric topologies~\cite{goodge2025stfmvision}.

Formally, a spatio-temporal system is represented as a spatial topology $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, \mathbf{A})$, where $\mathcal{V}$ denotes a set of $N = |\mathcal{V}|$ spatial sensor nodes or grid cells, $\mathcal{E}$ represents physical or learned relational edges, and $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the spatial adjacency matrix (or spatial distance metric). Given historical observations $\mathbf{X}_{1:T} = [\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_T] \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N \times D_{\text{in}}}$, the spatio-temporal foundation objective requires forecasting future states $\mathbf{X}_{T+1:T+H} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times N \times D_{\text{out}}}$ across both seen and entirely unseen spatial topologies $\mathcal{G}^*$:
\begin{equation}
    P(\mathbf{X}_{T+1:T+H} \mid \mathbf{X}_{1:T}, \mathcal{G}) = \prod_{h=1}^H P(\mathbf{x}_{T+h} \mid \mathbf{X}_{1:T+h-1}, \mathbf{A}).
\end{equation}

Traditional deep spatio-temporal architectures (e.g., STGCN, DCRNN) relied on fixed static spatial graph Laplacian operators and task-specific training, catastrophically failing when deployed zero-shot across new sensor networks or different urban geographies. Modern Spatio-Temporal Foundation Models (STFMs) circumvent this structural barrier through four primary paradigms:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Dual Spatio-Temporal Self-Attention with Graph Biases (OpenCity)}: Li et al.~\cite{li2024opencity} present OpenCity, an open-world foundation model for urban traffic forecasting. OpenCity decomposes representations into decoupled spatial and temporal transformer layers. To eliminate reliance on rigid fixed adjacency matrices, OpenCity injects generalized graph wavelet positional encodings and relative spatial distance embeddings into multi-head self-attention. Pre-trained on multi-city traffic networks comprising over $50\text{M}$ observations, OpenCity demonstrates remarkable zero-shot transfer across previously unobserved metropolitan road networks.
    \item \emph{Prompt-Empowered Universal Masked Autoencoding (UniST)}: Yuan et al.~\cite{yuan2024unist} introduce UniST (Tsinghua FIB Lab), moving beyond scenario-specific architectures. UniST formulates spatio-temporal forecasting through unified generative masked pretraining. To accommodate heterogeneous sampling rates, variable sensor densities, and semantic domain shifts (e.g., traffic velocity vs. pedestrian flow vs. bike sharing), UniST injects knowledge-guided continuous prompt tokens into the transformer backbone, guiding the shared model to dynamically adapt spatial-temporal priors without altering backbone parameters.
    \item \emph{Spatio-Temporal Diffusion Transformers (UrbanDiT)}: Li et al.~\cite{li2024urbandit} establish UrbanDiT, scaling Diffusion Transformers (DiT) to open-world spatio-temporal systems. UrbanDiT tokenizes both irregular spatial graphs and regular spatial raster grids into unified spatio-temporal patches. By combining reverse-time score-based diffusion denoising with task prompt embeddings, UrbanDiT unifies diverse downstream tasks—including bidirectional forecasting, spatial imputation, and trajectory extrapolation—under a single generative score network.
    \item \emph{Minimalist Scaling and Inductive Regularization (UrbanFM)}: Chen et al.~\cite{chen2026urbanfm} present UrbanFM, scaling spatio-temporal foundation models up to $120\text{M}$ parameters. UrbanFM challenges over-engineered spatial graph heuristics, proving that minimalist self-attention with constrained inductive biases and \emph{MiniST} tokenization (which normalizes variance across disparate city sensors) achieves superior scaling laws and cross-city zero-shot transfer compared to complex graph convolution-transformer hybrids.
    \item \emph{Satellite Image Time Series Foundation Models (TiMo)}: Qin et al.~\cite{qin2025timo} introduce TiMo, scaling spatio-temporal foundation architectures to Satellite Image Time Series (SITS). Departing from standard vision transformers that process static frames independently, TiMo incorporates a hierarchical spatio-temporal gyroscope attention mechanism that captures evolving multi-scale seasonal patterns across planetary terrain. Pre-trained on the \emph{MillionST} dataset comprising one million satellite image phases across 100,000 geographic locations over five years, TiMo unifies deforestation monitoring, crop classification, and flood risk segmentation under a single masked autoencoding foundation.
    \item \emph{Unified Conceptual Vision and Generalization Challenges}: Goodge et al.~\cite{goodge2025stfmvision} formulate a comprehensive taxonomy for STFMs, identifying four orthogonal dimensions of generalization required for true foundational capability: (1) \emph{Spatial Generalization} across varying graph topologies and geographic layouts; (2) \emph{Temporal Generalization} across diverse sampling resolutions and non-stationary distribution shifts; (3) \emph{Scale Generalization} across localized sensor clusters and regional macro-grids; and (4) \emph{Cross-Domain Generalization} bridging human mobility, climate phenomena, and cyber-physical infrastructure.
\end{itemize}
These advances demonstrate that spatial graphs and 1D temporal series can be harmoniously unified within scalable transformer and diffusion backbones, establishing spatio-temporal modeling as a vibrant pillar of modern foundation model research.

\subsection{Lightweight, Edge-Native, and Microcontroller TSFMs}
\label{sec:edge_native_tsfm}

While industrial-scale foundation models scale from hundreds of millions to billions of parameters, mission-critical operational technology (OT)—such as cyber-physical manufacturing loops, wireless telecommunications infrastructure, and embedded microcontrollers—operates under rigid constraints of sub-10ms response times, micro-watt energy budgets, and sub-100MB RAM ceilings. This has catalyzed a dedicated design movement toward \emph{edge-native foundation models}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Tiny-TSM (Birkel, 2025)}~\cite{birkel2025tinytsm}: Birkel presents Tiny-TSM, establishing that foundation capabilities can be synthesized within an ultra-compact 23M parameter footprint. Trained on a single consumer-accessible A100 GPU in less than one week using the synthetic \emph{SynthTS} generation pipeline, Tiny-TSM incorporates a novel causal input normalization scheme. This formulation enables dense next-token prediction loss without autoregressive rollout collapse, matching or outperforming industrial-scale billion-parameter foundation baselines across medium- and long-horizon forecasting tasks under mean squared error.
    \item \emph{APEX: Network-Native Wireless Telemetry (Pradhan et al., 2026)}~\cite{pradhan2026apex}: Developed at Cisco Systems, APEX addresses the failure mode where generic time series models falter on bursty, zero-inflated, and multi-layer coupled wireless network telemetry. APEX introduces a decoder-only transformer pre-trained on 10-channel telemetry spanning 4,500 production enterprise networks ($\approx 100\text{K}$ access point time series). It is released in two operational tiers: \emph{APEX-Large} ($269\text{M}$ parameters for cloud analytics, reducing MAE by $18\%$ over Toto~\cite{datadog2026toto2}) and \emph{APEX-Edge} ($10.5\text{M}$ parameters), which executes sub-second, privacy-preserving zero-shot anomaly detection directly on router/AP-class edge hardware.
    \item \emph{Ultra-Lightweight Patch-Independent MLPs (Cheraghinia et al., 2025)}~\cite{cheraghinia2025lightweight}: Cheraghinia et al. demonstrate that transformer self-attention is unnecessary for constrained edge devices. By deploying simple patch-independent multi-layer perceptron (MLP) encoders comprising a mere $21\text{K}$ trainable parameters, their foundation architecture executes downstream wireless modulation classification, technology recognition, and line-of-sight detection in $0.33\text{ms}$ on commodity microcontrollers, maintaining $>97\%$ accuracy across previously unseen data classes without representation degradation.
    \item \emph{MACROCAST: Vintage-Consistent Real-Time Foundations (Carriero et al., 2026)}~\cite{carriero2026macrocast}: Carriero, Pettenuzzo, and Shekhar formulate MACROCAST, an ultra-efficient 15M-parameter foundation model for real-time macroeconomic forecasting that systematically eliminates both temporal lookahead contamination and revision bias. Standard TSFMs trained on fully revised time series suffer from hindsight leakage because historical macroeconomic indicators (e.g., GDP, non-farm payrolls) undergo extensive post-hoc revisions unavailable to real-time decision-makers. MACROCAST rules out both leakage forms by pretraining on purely synthetic time series generated from Bayesian Vector Autoregressions (BVARs) and Dynamic Factor Models (DFMs) in one GPU-day, followed by nine-minute fine-tuning on vintage-specific ALFRED releases. On the real-time FRED-MD benchmark, MACROCAST beats AR(1) on $80\%$ of horizon pairs and surpasses Chronos-2~\cite{ansari2025chronos2} and classical BVARs without data leakage.
    \item \emph{TQS-PTQ: Dynamical Systems Quantization Sensitivity (Pavlova et al., 2026)}~\cite{pavlova2026tqs}: Pavlova et al. reframe post-training quantization (PTQ) for time-series foundation forecasters through the mathematical lens of dynamical systems stability. Conventional PTQ sensitivity heuristics are tightly coupled to specific quantizer bit-widths and calibration sets. In contrast, the Trajectory-based Quantization Sensitivity Score (TQS) models the autoregressive latent rollout as a discrete-time dynamical system $\mathbf{z}_{t+1} = \Phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{x}_t)$, rigorously bounding how local quantization perturbation errors amplify across the forecasting horizon. Decoupled from quantizer selection, TQS-PTQ derives an exact sensitivity budget across layers prior to quantization, enabling robust mixed-precision allocation (FP16 down to INT4) for compiled, black-box edge models without requiring calibration data or second-order Hessian approximations.
    \item \emph{Calibration Bets on the Past: 4-Bit Activation Calibration (Ye \& Wanjiku, 2026)}~\cite{ye2026calibration}: Ye and Wanjiku conduct an extensive empirical audit of post-training quantization across 560 neural forecasters and eight walk-forward testing years on S\&P 500 volatility series. Their findings demonstrate a fundamental dichotomy between weight and activation quantization: while 8-bit quantization is largely insensitive to calibration methodology, static 4-bit activation quantization causes severe degradation under standard absolute-maximum (abs-max) calibration, destroying 11\% to 62\% of predictive signal. Replacing abs-max with percentile calibration recovers 53\% to 94\% of this loss by effectively filtering temporal outliers and structural distribution breaks, establishing activation calibration as an indispensable prerequisite for 4-bit time-series deployment.
    \item \emph{On-Device Fully Quantized Training on Microcontrollers (MCU-FQT, Deutel et al., 2024)}~\cite{deutel2024mcutraining}: Deutel et al. break through the barrier of edge adaptability on bare-metal ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers. Addressing the severe resource constraints of microcontrollers ($<256\text{ KB}$ SRAM and $<1\text{ MB}$ Flash memory), they formulate Fully Quantized Training (FQT), executing both forward inference passes and backward gradient propagation entirely within 8-bit integer arithmetic. Paired with dynamic partial gradient updates that selectively update only high-sensitivity layers, MCU-FQT enables embedded sensor forecasters to continuously fine-tune to streaming distribution shifts on-device without floating-point emulation overhead.
    \item \emph{Sparse Binary Transformers: 1-Bit Weights for Multivariate Time Series (Gorbett et al., 2023)}~\cite{gorbett2023sparsebinary}: Gorbett, Shirazi, and Ray pioneer extreme 1-bit binary parameterization ($\mathbf{W} \in \{-1, +1\}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$) for multivariate time-series transformers. Rather than relying on floating-point matrix multiplications $\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}$, binary quantization transforms linear projections in multi-head self-attention and patch feedforward blocks into addition-only accumulation:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{x} = \sum_{j: W_{ij}=+1} x_j - \sum_{j: W_{ij}=-1} x_j.
    \end{equation}
    Coupled with learnable binary scaling vectors and multi-rate patch downsampling, Sparse Binary Transformers achieve an $87\%$ parameter compression ratio and an order-of-magnitude reduction in arithmetic logic unit (ALU) gate toggle rates on edge FPGAs and embedded ASICs, proving that temporal attention maps can be effectively learned under binary weight discretization.
    \item \emph{Q-DEQ: Quantized Deep Equilibrium Forecasters (Yang et al., 2026)}~\cite{yang2026qdeq}: Yang et al. address edge deployment coding constraints by bridging Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs) with low-bit quantization. Conventional deep architectures stack explicit feedforward layers, incurring memory footprints proportional to network depth. In contrast, DEQs simulate infinite effective depth by computing the implicit fixed-point equilibrium state:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}^* = f_\theta(\mathbf{z}^*, \mathbf{x}),
    \end{equation}
    using a single shared transformation layer ($0.3\text{M}$ parameters). Q-DEQ resolves the numerical divergence that standard fixed-point Broyden solvers experience under discrete INT4/INT8 arithmetic by formulating a discrete-solving relaxation and contraction-mapping boundary constraint. Q-DEQ achieves parity with full-precision FP32 PatchTST and DLinear baselines while cutting memory storage to $75\text{ KB}$, enabling continuous dynamical equilibrium modeling within microcontroller cache bounds.
    \item \emph{SGA: State-Guided Autoregressive Uncertainty Calibration (Hu et al., 2026)}~\cite{hu2026sga}: Hu et al. address the uncalibrated error accumulation inherent to long-horizon autoregressive rollouts in foundation forecasters. While multi-step forecasting models recursively feed predicted patches $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t$ back as context $\mathbf{x}_{t+1}$, epistemic errors cascade exponentially, producing miscalibrated predictive intervals. SGA formulates a State-Guided Autoregressive adapter: by extracting the foundation backbone's internal hidden transition trajectories $\mathbf{h}_t$ and conditioning a dual-headed Gaussian-process parametric head on state-space curvature, SGA dynamically scales rollout variance without requiring computationally prohibitive Monte Carlo sampling or ensemble inference.
\end{itemize}
These architectures prove that foundation pretraining is not intrinsically tied to massive parameter scales; structured inductive priors, synthetic data curation, and patch-level modularity enable sub-megabyte foundation models for extreme edge environments.

\subsection{Non-Stationary Temporal Drift and Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)}
\label{sec:test_time_adaptation}

A foundational vulnerability of all pre-trained temporal forecasters is the assumption of distributional stationarity between pretraining corpora $\mathcal{D}_{\text{pre}}$ and streaming inference horizons $\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$. In open cyber-physical, meteorological, and economic environments, time series are intrinsically non-stationary, exhibiting evolving mean-variance trajectories, seasonal structural breaks, and abrupt concept drift:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_{\text{train}}(\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}) \neq \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}(\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}), \quad \mathcal{P}_{\text{train}}(\mathbf{Y}_{t:t+H} \mid \mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}) \neq \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}(\mathbf{Y}_{t:t+H} \mid \mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}).
\end{equation}
While standard fine-tuning requires labeled supervision and risks catastrophic forgetting of general priors, \emph{Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)} enables pre-trained foundation models to continually adapt their latent representations to streaming distribution shifts using only unlabeled observations available at inference time~\cite{kim2025battling,dang2026adanodes}.

Recent advances formalize TTA for foundation time-series forecasters across three primary paradigms:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Online Test-Time Adaptation Forecasters (TSF-TTA)}: Kim et al.~\cite{kim2025battling} establish the TSF-TTA framework, systematically addressing non-stationarity in temporal models without requiring access to source pretraining datasets. TSF-TTA freezes the feature extraction backbone and restricts test-time updates to reversible instance normalization statistics and lightweight dynamic linear adapter heads. By minimizing an unsupervised temporal consistency objective across sliding test windows, TSF-TTA dynamically tracks concept drift, cutting test-time prediction error by up to $22\%$ across non-stationary benchmarks while incurring negligible compute overhead.
    \item \emph{Continuous Dynamical Adaptation via Neural ODEs (AdaNODEs)}: Dang et al.~\cite{dang2026adanodes} introduce AdaNODEs, moving beyond discrete heuristic gradient steps by formulating test-time parameter trajectories through continuous-time Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs). In AdaNODEs, the evolution of latent state representations $\mathbf{z}(t)$ under streaming temporal drift is governed by a parameterized continuous vector field:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{d\mathbf{z}(t)}{dt} = f_{\theta_{\text{ode}}}(\mathbf{z}(t), t), \quad \mathbf{z}(t_1) = \mathbf{z}(t_0) + \int_{t_0}^{t_1} f_{\theta_{\text{ode}}}(\mathbf{z}(\tau), \tau) \, d\tau.
    \end{equation}
    By updating only an ultra-compact $0.15\text{M}$ parameter NODE adapter rather than backbone weights, AdaNODEs smoothly accommodates irregular sampling intervals and continuous distribution shifts, demonstrating superior parameter efficiency and convergence stability over standard gradient descent.
    \item \emph{Regime-Guided Meta-Control (RG-TTA)}: Kumar et al.~\cite{kumar2026rgtta} resolve the dilemma between under-adaptation (failing to catch sudden shifts) and over-adaptation (overfitting to transient noise). RG-TTA introduces a regime-guided meta-controller that continuously computes rolling distribution statistics (spectral energy, mean drift, variance change) to classify the streaming series into stationary baseline vs. shifted regimes. The meta-controller dynamically modulates the adaptation learning rate $\eta_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_r \mathbf{r}_t + b_r)$ and gradient clipping thresholds, applying aggressive updates during genuine regime changes while damping updates during stable periods to preserve foundational knowledge.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Causal Discovery, Structural Priors, and Counterfactual Foundation Models}
\label{sec:causal_foundation}

While contemporary time series foundation models exhibit remarkable correlational forecasting capabilities, their black-box representations struggle with out-of-distribution intervention reasoning, policy simulation, and root-cause attribution~\cite{jander2026causal}. Integrating \emph{Structural Causal Models (SCMs)} into foundation architectures represents a crucial frontier bridging deep representations with causal mechanics~\cite{stein2024embracing,thumm2026interventional}.

Formally, a dynamic temporal structural causal model defines a multivariate time series $\mathbf{X}_t = [X_{1,t}, \dots, X_{D,t}]^\top$ across time steps $t$ via a system of structural equations over parent variables $\text{PA}(X_{i,t})$ and independent exogenous noise processes $U_{i,t}$:
\begin{equation}
    X_{i,t} := f_i\left(\text{PA}_{\text{inter}}(X_{i,t}), \text{PA}_{\text{intra}}(X_{i,t}), U_{i,t}\right), \quad U_{i,t} \sim \mathcal{P}_{U_i},
\end{equation}
where $\text{PA}_{\text{inter}}(X_{i,t}) \subseteq \{X_{j,t-\tau} \mid j \in [D], \tau \ge 1\}$ denotes lagged inter-temporal causal parents and $\text{PA}_{\text{intra}}(X_{i,t}) \subseteq \{X_{j,t} \mid j \neq i\}$ represents instantaneous intra-temporal parents, defining an underlying causal Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) $\mathcal{G}_{\text{causal}}$.

Recent foundation architectures synthesize causality across three complementary frontiers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Foundation Models for Time-Series Causal Discovery (Causal-PT)}: Stein et al.~\cite{stein2024embracing} demonstrate that causal DAG discovery from temporal signals can be learned end-to-end by a pre-trained foundation transformer. Rather than relying on combinatorial constraint-based testing (e.g., PCMCI) or continuous optimization (e.g., DYNOTEARS), Causal-PT pre-trains a 25M-parameter transformer over 10 million synthetically generated nonlinear structural causal processes. At test time, Causal-PT maps raw multivariate temporal trajectories directly to the estimated causal adjacency matrix $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ in a single feedforward pass, outperforming classical algorithms while exhibiting high robustness to observational noise.
    \item \emph{Interventional Time Series Priors for In-Context Causal Models (CausalTimePrior)}: Thumm and Chen~\cite{thumm2026interventional} pioneer the fusion of Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs) with temporal structural causality. Prior PFNs (e.g., TabPFN, ForecastPFN) were constrained strictly to observational data. CausalTimePrior constructs a synthetic generator producing hundreds of millions of diverse temporal structural causal models (TSCMs) paired with both observational trajectories and atomic interventions $do(X_j = \alpha)$. By pre-training a transformer on these paired trajectories, CausalTimePrior performs exact in-context causal effect estimation and counterfactual forecasting in a single forward evaluation without requiring gradient optimization.
    \item \emph{Causal Time Series Generation via Diffusion Models (CaTSG)}: Xia et al.~\cite{xia2025causal} introduce CaTSG, establishing a structural causal diffusion framework for multivariate time series. CaTSG incorporates time-varying causal graph representations into the reverse-time score-matching process:
    \begin{equation}
        s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, \mathcal{G}_{\text{causal}}) \approx \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathcal{G}_{\text{causal}}).
    \end{equation}
    By constraining the generative diffusion trajectories through learned causal adjacency masks, CaTSG guarantees that generated trajectories strictly adhere to physical causal dependencies, enabling controllable simulation across observational, interventional, and counterfactual query spaces.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Continuous-Time Asynchronous Event Streams and Market Microstructure Foundations}
\label{sec:asynchronous_event_foundations}

A fundamental limitation of conventional time series foundation models lies in their rigid presumption of uniform, synchronous discrete sampling grids ($\Delta t = \text{const}$). In real-world environments---such as financial limit order books (LOBs), electronic health records, and seismic sensor networks---observations arrive as continuous-time asynchronous event streams $\mathcal{S} = \{(t_i, \mathbf{m}_i)\}_{i=1}^N$, where inter-arrival intervals $\tau_i = t_i - t_{i-1} \in (0, \infty)$ span multiple orders of magnitude and marks $\mathbf{m}_i \in \mathcal{M}$ encode heterogeneous event features. Imposing artificial uniform binning incurs substantial temporal distortion, aliasing high-frequency bursts while creating sparse, computationally wasteful zero-padded windows during quiescent periods.

Formally, an asynchronous event sequence is governed by a marked temporal point process characterized by a conditional intensity function $\lambda^*(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0^+} \frac{\mathbb{P}(\text{event in } [t, t+\Delta t) \mid \mathcal{H}_t)}{\Delta t}$, where $\mathcal{H}_t$ represents the filtration of historical events up to time $t$. Foundation modeling over such continuous-time streams requires architectural invariants capable of handling arbitrary time deltas and cross-dataset intensity scale mismatches:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{SurF: Generative Continuous-Time Foundation Models via Time Rescaling (Rezaei et al., 2026)}~\cite{rezaei2026surf}: Rezaei, Balaji, and Krishnan resolve the intractable quadrature integration problem in neural temporal point processes by leveraging the classical \emph{Time Rescaling Theorem (TRT)} as a learnable bijective continuous flow. The TRT establishes that any point process with continuous cumulative intensity $\Lambda(t) = \int_0^t \lambda^*(s) ds$ can be transformed into a standard unit-rate Poisson process:
    \begin{equation}
        \xi_i = \Lambda(t_i) - \Lambda(t_{i-1}) \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \text{Exp}(1).
    \end{equation}
    SurF parameterizes $\Lambda_\theta(t)$ via monotonic neural networks and inverts this transformation: during training, event inter-arrival times are mapped into standardized exponential noise, while generative sampling draws $\xi_i \sim \text{Exp}(1)$ and computes $t_i = \Lambda_\theta^{-1}(\Lambda_\theta(t_{i-1}) + \xi_i)$ in closed form. Crucially, mapping heterogeneous streams to a unified unit-rate latent space enables multi-dataset joint pretraining across earthquakes, retweets, and e-commerce streams, eliminating loss scale incompatibilities and establishing the first scalable foundation model for continuous irregular event forecasting.
    \item \emph{TradeFM: Generative Market Microstructure Foundation Models (Kawawa-Beaudan et al., 2026)}~\cite{kawawabeaudan2026tradefm}: Developed at J.P. Morgan AI Research, TradeFM introduces a 524M-parameter generative transformer foundation model trained on billions of asynchronous order-flow events spanning over 9,000 global equities. TradeFM addresses the severe cross-asset non-stationarity of financial microstructure by introducing scale-invariant universal tokenization: order book depths, trade volumes, and micro-price updates are mapped into normalized continuous event embeddings decoupled from idiosyncratic stock price scales. Coupled with a high-fidelity deterministic market matching engine, TradeFM performs zero-shot generative simulation of market dynamics, accurately replicating non-linear stylized facts (volatility clustering, heavy-tailed return distributions, and order-flow autocorrelation decay) while generalizing zero-shot across distinct international geographic regimes (e.g., European and APAC exchanges).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Physics-Informed Foundations and Scientific Constraint Injection}
\label{sec:physics_informed_foundations}

In mission-critical physical and industrial domains---such as electric power grids, aerospace aerodynamics, and renewable energy generation---purely data-driven foundation models face severe adoption barriers: their unconstrained predictions frequently violate invariant physical conservation laws (e.g., Kirchhoff's laws, energy conservation, power balance constraints), leading to catastrophic failures if deployed in autonomous control loops. Embedding physical inductors directly into foundation architectures and pretraining pipelines represents an essential paradigm for verifiable scientific foundation models:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{GridSFM: Physics-Inspired Foundation Models for Power Flow (Bhan et al., 2026)}~\cite{bhan2026gridsfm}: Bhan et al. develop GridSFM, a 15M-parameter physics-inspired foundation model for solving AC Optimal Power Flow (AC-OPF) across large-scale electrical transmission grids. Rather than treating power flow as generic spatio-temporal regression, GridSFM embeds the non-linear AC power balance equations:
    \begin{equation}
        S_i = V_i \sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}_i} Y_{ik}^* V_k^*, \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{bus}},
    \end{equation}
    directly into a physics-guided graph neural network. To address non-convex feasibility boundaries and disconnected solution manifolds, GridSFM introduces a lifted problem reformulation with logarithmically penalized slack variables. Pretrained over 54 distinct transmission topologies (500 to 4,000 buses), GridSFM achieves a remarkable $2.45\%$ zero-shot generation-cost error when evaluated on unseen 10,000-bus power grids, providing warm-start trajectories that accelerate commercial interior-point solvers by over an order of magnitude.
    \item \emph{Physics-Informed Synthetic Histories for Cold-Start Forecasting (Longarini et al., 2026)}~\cite{longarini2026physics}: Longarini et al. tackle the severe ``cold-start'' barrier in renewable energy forecasting, where newly commissioned solar photovoltaic (PV) facilities lack historical telemetry required for foundation model in-context conditioning. The authors introduce a two-stage physics-informed pipeline: deterministic physical model chains (incorporating solar positioning, irradiance transposition, and thermal cell equations via \texttt{pvlib}) construct synthetic production histories $\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{1:T}$ solely from geographical coordinates and numerical weather predictions. Feeding these physically consistent synthetic histories into off-the-shelf foundation models (TabPFN-TS, Chronos-2, TimesFM) without fine-tuning reduces forecasting error (MAE) by up to $50\%$ compared to classical statistical models across 440 commercial installations, demonstrating that physics-based surrogate conditioning bridges data scarcity in zero-shot foundation deployments.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hierarchical Spatio-Temporal Graph Foundations and Geometric Mesh Manifolds}
\label{sec:hierarchical_graph_manifolds}

While classic time-series architectures assume independent channels or uniform Euclidean grids, real-world physical and socio-economic systems operate over complex, non-Euclidean geometric networks—such as continental river basins, urban traffic graphs, decentralized financial protocols, and spherical atmospheric grids. Formally, a spatio-temporal graph is defined as $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, \mathbf{A})$, where $\mathcal{V} = \{v_1, \dots, v_N\}$ is a set of nodes, $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V}$ is a set of dynamic or static edges characterized by adjacency matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, and each node generates a temporal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$. A foundation model over spatio-temporal manifolds must jointly solve two coupled challenges: (1) learning message-passing representations invariant to permutation and graph relabeling across disparate topologies; and (2) handling extreme temporal sparsity and non-uniform sampling across irregular physical boundaries:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{GPT-ST: Generative Spatio-Temporal Graph Pre-Training (Li et al., 2023)}~\cite{li2023gptst}: Accepted at NeurIPS 2023, Li et al. formulate GPT-ST, establishing the first standardized generative pre-training framework for spatio-temporal graph neural networks. GPT-ST introduces a spatio-temporal masked autoencoder coupled with customized parameter learners and hierarchical spatial pattern encoding networks. By dynamically clustering road networks into hierarchical region semantics (intra-cluster and inter-cluster), GPT-ST trains with an adaptive easy-to-hard spatio-temporal masking curriculum. The resulting representations seamlessly transfer to downstream forecasting baselines across multi-city PeMS traffic sensor networks with substantial performance boosts.
    \item \emph{AmazonSWE: Continental-Scale Hydrology on Ultra-Sparse DAG Manifolds (Cartuyvels et al., 2026)}~\cite{cartuyvels2026hydrology}: Cartuyvels et al. address one of the most demanding physical graph challenges: continental monitoring of water surface elevation across river networks. Introducing the AmazonSWE dataset—spanning over 19,000 river reaches over 10 years (2016--2026) in the Amazon basin utilizing NASA/CNES Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) satellite radar altimetry—the authors formalize the directed acyclic river topology as a massive spatiotemporal graph with extreme sparsity ($<1\%$ of river sections observed on any given day). To overcome the computational collapse of classical GNNs on this topology, the authors propose a bidirectional selective state-space model that samples connected subgraphs and flattens space and time into a single sequence equipped with topological directed acyclic graph (DAG) positional encodings. This formulation cuts root-mean-square error (RMSE) against held-out in-situ river gauges by $18\%\text{--}39\%$ compared to hybrid physical-statistical hydrodynamic models.
    \item \emph{DeXposure-FM: Time-Series Graph Foundation Model for Decentralized Financial Networks (Shu et al., 2026)}~\cite{shu2026dexposure}: Shu et al. introduce DeXposure-FM, the first graph foundation model engineered to quantify systemic financial contagion and credit exposure on decentralized finance (DeFi) networks. Trained on the massive DeXposure corpus comprising 43.7 million transaction records across 4,300+ protocols on 602 blockchains (encompassing 24,300+ unique tokens), DeXposure-FM employs a graph-tabular encoder with pre-trained initialization. The foundation architecture jointly predicts multi-horizon protocol liquidity flows and dynamic inter-protocol dependency edge weights, supporting macroprudential stress testing and systemic risk scoring across interconnected blockchain economies.
    \item \emph{EarthGeometry: Physically Typed and Geometry-Aware Planetary Representations (Ranjan, 2026)}~\cite{ranjan2026earthgeometry}: Addressing planetary Earth foundation models, Ranjan establishes a rigorous formalization of geometric invariance and physical typing. While standard geospatial embeddings treat data as arbitrary vectors in $\mathbb{R}^d$, physical atmospheric and oceanic fields possess strict transformation laws under spatial rotations, reflections, and coordinate chart changes. Ranjan demonstrates that planetary models must explicitly distinguish between scalar fields (temperature, pressure), tangent vector fields (horizontal wind velocities), axial pseudovectors (vorticity, magnetic fields), and higher-order stress tensors. By integrating Hodge and Helmholtz decompositions over spherical geodesic meshes, this framework proves that geometry-aware physical typing substantially improves out-of-distribution generalization across planetary weather forecasting regimes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Selective State-Space Models (Mamba/S4) and Linear-Time Sequence Modeling}
\label{sec:ssm_mamba_foundations}

While multi-head self-attention has powered the majority of early TSFMs, its quadratic computational complexity $O(L^2)$ and quadratic memory footprint with respect to sequence length $L$ pose an insurmountable bottleneck for ultra-long context windows and resource-constrained edge deployments. To overcome this limitation, continuous-time Structured State-Space Models (SSMs) and Selective State-Space Models (Mamba) have emerged as potent alternatives. Formally, a continuous state-space model maps a 1D continuous sequence $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ to $y(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ through an intermediate hidden state $\mathbf{h}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\mathbf{h}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{h}(t) + \mathbf{B}x(t), \quad y(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{h}(t) + \mathbf{D}x(t).
\end{equation}
Discretization via Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) across sampling intervals $\Delta_t$ yields discrete recurrence matrices:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\mathbf{A}}_t = \exp(\Delta_t \mathbf{A}), \quad \bar{\mathbf{B}}_t = (\Delta_t \mathbf{A})^{-1}(\exp(\Delta_t \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{I})\Delta_t \mathbf{B}_t.
\end{equation}
In modern selective state-space architectures, $\mathbf{B}_t, \mathbf{C}_t,$ and the timescale parameter $\Delta_t$ are dynamic, input-dependent projections of the current token $x_t$. This allows the model to filter out irrelevant temporal noise while compressing arbitrarily long histories into a fixed-dimensional state $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with linear $O(L)$ training time and constant $O(1)$ memory per step during autoregressive generation. Pioneering works have established the foundation validity of SSMs for time series:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{S-Mamba: Is Mamba Effective for Time Series Forecasting? (Wang et al., 2024)}~\cite{wang2024smamba}: Wang et al. formulate S-Mamba to rigorously investigate whether selective state-space models can supplant self-attention in multivariate time-series forecasting. While Transformers excel at pairwise feature comparison, their attention weights often degrade on periodic, noise-dominated signals. S-Mamba introduces bidirectional Mamba blocks operating across both temporal patch sequences (intra-series modeling) and multivariate channels (inter-series cross-variable correlation). S-Mamba matches or exceeds state-of-the-art transformer baselines across ETT, Electricity, and Weather benchmarks while delivering linear memory scaling and $3\times\text{--}5\times$ speedups on long sequences.
    \item \emph{TimeMachine: A Time Series is Worth 4 Mambas (Ahamed and Cheng, 2024)}~\cite{ahamed2024timemachine}: Accepted at ECAI 2024, Ahamed and Cheng propose TimeMachine, a quadruplet 4-Mamba architecture explicitly designed for long-term forecasting. Recognizing that time series exhibit complex dual-scale dynamics, TimeMachine deploys four interconnected Mamba modules that simultaneously decouple and route representations across two orthogonal axes: (1) forward and backward temporal sequences; and (2) intra-channel local subseries and inter-channel global interactions. TimeMachine maintains a strictly linear memory footprint, resolving GPU out-of-memory failures on context windows exceeding $10,000$ timesteps.
    \item \emph{Bi-Mamba+: Bidirectional Selective State Space for Time Series (Liang et al., 2024)}~\cite{liang2024bimamba}: A critical drawback of standard causal SSMs is their unidirectional scan, which inevitably discards future-looking contextual correlation in offline forecasting scenarios. Liang et al. introduce Bi-Mamba+, which interleaves forward and backward selective state-space layers with adaptive hidden-state fusion. By processing historical patches in both temporal directions, Bi-Mamba+ eliminates unidirectional bias without incurring the quadratic computational cost of bidirectional full-attention encoders.
    \item \emph{QuantFlow: Federated Mamba Post-Transformer Foundation Models (Haider et al., 2026)}~\cite{haider2026quantflow}: Addressing edge-constrained distributed environments, Haider et al. introduce QuantFlow, a post-transformer federated foundation model built entirely upon selective state-space backbones. In federated forecasting across decentralized IoT sensors and medical telemetry, transmitting large Transformer KV-caches and dense attention weights induces severe communication bottlenecks. QuantFlow replaces self-attention with lightweight quantized Mamba blocks, allowing multiple distributed edge nodes to train collaborative global foundations with $70\%$ lower memory footprint and zero raw-data leakage.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Heterogeneous Multivariate Decoupling, Covariate Homogenization, and Memory-Augmented Adaptation}
\label{sec:heterogeneous_multivariate_memory}

A pervasive architectural limitation of first-generation TSFMs is their reliance on channel independence or homogeneous Euclidean embeddings. In real-world industrial, environmental, and financial deployments, multivariate time series exhibit severe cross-channel heterogeneity: physical variables possess distinct measurement units, sampling dynamics, and both synergistic and antagonistic interactions. Furthermore, time-series series are accompanied by heterogeneous exogenous covariates (e.g., discrete calendar events, static metadata, continuous weather streams, and unstructured text logs). Naive joint attention across heterogeneous variables frequently induces \emph{negative transfer} and \emph{representation distortion}, where dominant variables swamp subtle leading indicators, or where fine-tuning a universal backbone destroys its zero-shot generalization capabilities. Recent foundation models from 2025--2026 systematically address these structural challenges:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Falcon-X: Unified Prototype Diff-Attention and Variate Reassembly (Liu et al., 2026)}~\cite{liu2026falconx}: Liu et al. formulate Falcon-X (230M parameters), establishing a foundation paradigm designed specifically for heterogeneous multivariate systems. Recognizing that standard softmax attention enforces non-negative affinity weights that cannot capture antagonistic or competing physical dynamics (e.g., supply vs. price, temperature vs. cooling efficiency), Falcon-X introduces \emph{Unified Prototype Diff-Attention (UPDA)}. UPDA decouples raw variates from physical space and projects them into a shared latent prototype space $\mathcal{Z} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, computing differential affinities:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{A}_{\text{diff}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}_+^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) - \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}_-^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{K}_+$ and $\mathbf{K}_-$ parameterize positive synergistic and negative antagonistic prototype manifolds. Subsequently, a \emph{Variate Reassembly Router (VRR)} operates via a request-and-dispatch mechanism, querying prototype trajectories to reconstruct variate-specific predictions. Falcon-X achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization across 12 multivariate benchmarks, eliminating channel interference and reducing multivariate forecasting error by $17.4\%$ over channel-independent baselines.

    \item \emph{UniCA: Unified Covariate Adaptation via Homogenization (Han et al., 2025)}~\cite{han2025unica}: While foundation models are overwhelmingly pretrained on pure numerical series, production forecasting requires conditioning on heterogeneous covariates (e.g., holiday indicators, continuous price promotions, and categorical regional IDs). Han et al. propose UniCA (120M parameters), introducing the principle of \emph{Covariate Homogenization}. Rather than concatenating disparate raw covariates into the foundation backbone, UniCA maps all categorical, temporal, and multimodal covariate streams into unified, high-level continuous latent series through dedicated converter modules:
    \begin{equation}
        \tilde{\mathbf{C}} = \text{Converter}(\mathbf{C}_{\text{cat}}, \mathbf{C}_{\text{cont}}, \mathbf{C}_{\text{text}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_c}.
    \end{equation}
    UniCA then employs modular Pre-Fusion and Post-Fusion cross-attention blocks that inject the homogenized covariate representations into the frozen foundation backbone without corrupting its pretrained temporal representations. On GIFT-Eval and fev-bench covariate tracks, UniCA improves zero-shot accuracy by up to $22.1\%$ while preserving the foundation model's zero-shot generalization envelope.

    \item \emph{TS-Memory: Plug-and-Play Memory Distillation (Lyu et al., KDD 2026)}~\cite{lyu2026tsmemory}: Lyu et al. resolve the fundamental dilemma between parametric fine-tuning (which risks catastrophic forgetting and negative transfer) and non-parametric retrieval (which introduces prohibitive latency and retrieval noise during streaming inference). TS-Memory introduces a lightweight ($0.5\text{M}$ parameter) plug-and-play transformer memory adapter. During training, an offline, leakage-safe $k\text{NN}$ teacher retrieves historical neighbor futures from a target domain datastore, formulating confidence-weighted quantile correction targets. TS-Memory then distills these non-parametric insights into the parametric adapter using a \emph{confidence-gated loss}:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} = \sum_{t=1}^H w_t \cdot \rho_\tau\left(\hat{y}_{t}^{(\text{adapter})} - y_{t}^{(k\text{NN})}\right), \quad w_t = \sigma\left(\frac{\mu_t^{(k\text{NN})} - \text{Var}_t}{\kappa}\right),
    \end{equation}
    where $w_t$ selectively halts distillation whenever teacher retrieval uncertainty is high, strictly preventing negative transfer. During online inference, TS-Memory fuses with frozen backbones (e.g., Chronos-Bolt) in constant $O(1)$ time without database lookups, consistently outperforming full fine-tuning across distribution-shifted target domains.

    \item \emph{iAmTime: Instruction-Conditioned In-Context Meta-Learning (Saha and Shmakov, 2026)}~\cite{saha2026iamtime}: Developed by Walmart researchers, iAmTime (350M parameters) redefines foundation model adaptation by abandoning parameter fine-tuning in favor of \emph{instruction-conditioned in-context learning}. Conditioned on explicit input-output demonstration pairs $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{demo}}^{(i)}, \mathbf{Y}_{\text{demo}}^{(i)})_{i=1}^K$, iAmTime infers task intent on-the-fly. The architecture integrates a Hierarchical Multi-Scope Transformer Encoder (extracting multi-resolution temporal dynamics and latent task embeddings) with a Task-Conditioned Patch Decoder that routes representations through specialized expert heads. Pretrained across a massive corpus of over 120 billion real and synthetic observations, iAmTime executes zero-shot task switching across point forecasting, quantile prediction, signal inpainting, and classification without updating a single weight parameter.

    \item \emph{ST-Prune: Complexity-Aware Spatio-Temporal Dynamic Sample Pruning (Chen et al., 2026)}~\cite{chen2026stprune}: Addressing the unsustainable computational overhead of pretraining spatio-temporal foundation models on redundant planetary sensor grids, Chen et al. formulate ST-Prune. ST-Prune tracks the model's instantaneous sample-level loss gradients and temporal complexity in real time, dynamically pruning repetitive, low-information spatio-temporal slices during training. By allocating GPU compute strictly to high-entropy, topologically challenging boundary trajectories, ST-Prune cuts pretraining wall-clock time by $46\%$ while matching or exceeding the downstream generalization accuracy of full-dataset pretraining.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Active Sensor Querying, Information Acquisition, and Temporal Causal PFNs}
\label{sec:active_sensing_causal_pfns}

Deploying foundation models across real-world physical sensor grids (e.g., environmental observation, smart manufacturing, and autonomous vehicle fleets) encounters severe communication bandwidth and power constraints. Sensors cannot transmit continuous telemetry at high frequencies indefinitely; instead, models must actively decide \emph{which sensors to query}, \emph{at which time steps}, and \emph{when to defer to costly human experts or high-fidelity numerical solvers}. This necessitates unifying foundation representations with active information-theoretic acquisition functions and temporal causal discovery:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{STAP: Selective Time-Step Acquisition for Physical Dynamics (Kim et al., ICML 2025)}~\cite{kim2025stap}: Kim et al. formulate active learning for physical spatio-temporal trajectories governed by partial differential equations (PDEs; Navier-Stokes, reaction-diffusion). Traditional active learning queries full trajectories, incurring prohibitive solver costs. STAP introduces \emph{selective time-step acquisition}, using a foundation surrogate model to approximate easy trajectories and selectively acquiring only the most informative non-uniform time-steps $t_k \in [0, T]$ from the numerical solver. STAP derives an acquisition function based on expected global variance reduction:
    \begin{equation}
        \alpha(t^*) = \arg\max_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{cand}}} \mathbb{E}\left[ \text{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{post}}(\mathbf{x}_{1:T}) \mid y_t\right) \right] - \text{Cost}(t),
    \end{equation}
    enabling the surrogate model to explore a $3.4\times$ wider variety of nonlinear dynamical regimes under a fixed computational budget.

    \item \emph{L2D-SLDS: Online Learning-to-Defer via Switching State-Space Models (Montreuil et al., 2026)}~\cite{montreuil2026l2dslds}: Montreuil et al. formulate the first online Learning-to-Defer (L2D) framework for non-stationary streaming sensor networks. In critical industrial applications, the model must dynamically route each time step either to its internal foundation predictor or defer to an external specialist. L2D-SLDS tracks prediction residuals using a factorized Switching Linear-Gaussian State-Space Model (SLDS) decomposing dynamics into discrete regimes $s_t$, a global shared factor $\mathbf{z}_t$, and sensor-specific idiosyncratic noise $\mathbf{\epsilon}_t$. The decision to defer is governed by a learner-aware information query score:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{Q}_t = \underbrace{c_{\text{defer}}}_{\text{Immediate Cost}} - \underbrace{\lambda_1 \mathbb{I}\left(y_t; \mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathcal{H}_{t-1}\right)}_{\text{Latent Information Gain}} - \underbrace{\lambda_2 \Delta \mathcal{R}_t(\theta)}_{\text{Learner Regret Reduction}},
    \end{equation}
    which achieves superior tracking in non-stationary streaming environments with deferral rates under $2\%$.

    \item \emph{TCPFN: Temporal Causal Prior-Data Fitted Networks (Talupula and Sharma, 2026)}~\cite{talupula2026tcpfn}: Talupula and Sharma extend Prior-data Fitted Networks to temporal causal discovery on high-dimensional industrial panel series. While classic causal discovery algorithms (e.g., PCMCI, Granger causality) scale cubically $O(N^3)$ with variable count and fail under latent confounding, TCPFN trains a discrete-token panel transformer entirely on synthetic Structural Causal Models (SCMs) exhibiting delayed, decaying, and oscillating causal couplings. TCPFN introduces an explicit \emph{Causal Judgment Head} that outputs calibrated reliability signals: joint predictions of null-effect probability, confounding strength, and mediation fraction for every candidate treatment-outcome pair. Operating entirely zero-shot without per-dataset parameter updates, TCPFN processes industrial systems with thousands of time series in seconds, outperforming constraint-based and score-based discovery baselines on Tennessee Eastman and SWaT benchmarks.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Online Continual Learning, Temporal Plasticity, and Black-Box Residual Adaptation}
\label{sec:online_continual_plasticity}

While foundation models exhibit strong zero-shot generalization across diverse stationary datasets, real-world deployments encounter continuous, non-stationary temporal drift ($\mathcal{D}_t \neq \mathcal{D}_{t-1}$) where offline weights inevitably become stale. Furthermore, as foundation models are increasingly monetized as closed-source black-box API endpoints, standard backpropagation and white-box parameter fine-tuning become technically impossible. Recent 2025--2026 advances address this fundamental deployment frontier through online continual learning, temporal plasticity preservation, and black-box error-context adaptation:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{ELF: Efficient Lightweight Forecast-Adaption (Lee et al., ICML 2025)}~\cite{lee2025elf}: Published at ICML 2025, Lee et al. address the high computational barrier of adapting frozen foundation models to streaming feedback. They formulate ELF, a modular, model-agnostic adaptation mechanism operating alongside frozen backbones (e.g., Chronos, TimesFM, MOIRAI). ELF bifurcates online adaptation into two lightweight components: (1) the \emph{ELF-Forecaster}, a parameter-efficient frequency-domain module that rapidly learns localized distribution shifts from streaming errors, and (2) the \emph{ELF-Weighter}, an adaptive gating network that dynamically computes convex combination weights:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{\mathbf{y}}_t = w_t \hat{\mathbf{y}}_t^{\text{fast}} + (1 - w_t) \hat{\mathbf{y}}_t^{\text{FM}}, \quad w_t = \sigma\left(\mathbf{W}_w [\mathbf{x}_t; \hat{\mathbf{y}}_t^{\text{FM}}; \hat{\mathbf{y}}_t^{\text{fast}}]\right).
    \end{equation}
    By continuously calibrating $w_t$ against streaming validation loss, ELF prevents catastrophic representation collapse while delivering consistent $12\%\text{--}28\%$ error reductions over static foundation forecasts at negligible edge compute overhead.

    \item \emph{ORCA: Learning the Context of Errors for Black-Box TSFMs (Dai et al., NeurIPS 2026)}~\cite{dai2026orca}: Confronting commercial API boundaries where internal foundation parameters and gradients are completely inaccessible, Dai et al. formulate ORCA (Online Residual Contextual Adaptation). They identify a core flaw in conventional residual modeling: treating forecast errors $\mathbf{e}_t = \mathbf{y}_t - \hat{\mathbf{y}}_t^{\text{FM}}$ as unstructured white noise. Instead, ORCA proves that residual errors are structurally conditioned on both the input trajectory and the base model's prediction, defining the \emph{context of errors} $\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{e}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{1:t}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_t^{\text{FM}})$. ORCA trains a linear adapter over a temporal replay buffer with exponential historical forgetting decay $\gamma^k$, employing a predictive-space Bayesian loss:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{ORCA}} = \sum_{k=0}^K \gamma^k \left\| \mathbf{y}_{t-k} - \left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{t-k}^{\text{FM}} + \sum_{j=1}^M \frac{\exp(-\beta \|\mathbf{x}_{t-k} - \mathbf{c}_j\|^2)}{\sum_l \exp(-\beta \|\mathbf{x}_{t-k} - \mathbf{c}_l\|^2)} \hat{\mathbf{e}}_{t-k}^{(j)}\right) \right\|_2^2,
    \end{equation}
    where a Boltzmann router partitions error distributions across localized dynamical regimes. Evaluated across 5 leading TSFMs (Chronos-Bolt, TimesFM, Time-MoE) on 8 real-world streaming benchmarks, ORCA reduces streaming tracking error by up to $34.2\%$ without requiring a single backward gradient pass through the foundation model.

    \item \emph{NatSR: Natural Score-Driven Replay for Continual Forecasting (Urettini et al., ICLR 2026)}~\cite{urettini2026natsr}: Urettini et al. reformulate online continual learning for time series through the lens of parameter filtering. They show that standard gradient descent over non-stationary streams induces severe gradient noise that distorts long-term representations. NatSR introduces Natural Score-driven Replay, demonstrating that natural gradient descent corresponds to a score-driven updating scheme when optimizing conditional likelihoods. By framing predictions under a Student-$t$ distribution with degrees of freedom $\nu$:
    \begin{equation}
        \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t + \eta \mathbf{F}_t^{-1} \nabla_\theta \log p(y_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t, \theta_t), \quad p(y \mid \mu, \sigma^2, \nu) \propto \left(1 + \frac{(y - \mu)^2}{\nu \sigma^2}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}},
    \end{equation}
    NatSR endows foundation transformers with natural robustness against extreme temporal anomalies and spikes, while a dynamic scale heuristic selectively populates the replay memory during confirmed regime shifts, mitigating catastrophic forgetting across long-horizon streaming sequences.

    \item \emph{Temporal Plasticity in Billion-Scale Foundation Models (Liu et al., IJCNN 2025)}~\cite{liu2025temporalplasticity}: Liu et al. conduct the first rigorous empirical evaluation of \emph{temporal plasticity}—the capacity of neural networks to continually absorb non-stationary distributional shifts without suffering irreversible loss of learning capacity—in billion-scale foundation models (Time-MoE 2.4B, Chronos-Large). While compact task-specific networks rapidly suffer from representation stiffness and gradient vanishing during incremental fine-tuning, Liu et al. discover an emergent scaling property: billion-scale TSFMs preserve internal plasticity significantly longer, retaining broad cross-domain transferable representations while adapting efficiently to streaming domain shifts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-Sensory Foundations: Multi-Modal Industrial Telemetry and Sensor-Agnostic Tactile Policies}
\label{sec:cross_sensory_tactile_industrial}

A critical frontier in time series foundation models is transcending simple 1D scalar waveforms to encompass heterogeneous physical telemetry, including high-frequency acoustic vibrations, multi-level power grid dynamics, high-frequency robotic tactile contact streams, and self-supervised latent energy representations:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{FISHER: Multi-Modal Industrial Signal Representation (Fan et al., IEEE TII 2025)}~\cite{fan2025fisher}: Accepted for publication in IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics, Fan et al. tackle the severe heterogeneity of industrial asset monitoring, characterized as the ``M5 problem'' (multi-source, multi-rate, multi-modal, multi-condition, and multi-fault telemetry). To handle variable sampling rates across vibration, acoustic emissions, current, and dynamic pressure without lossy interpolation or resampling, FISHER introduces \emph{sub-band spectral tokenization}: treating sampling rate increments as concatenated sub-band orthogonal channels. Pretrained via teacher-student contrastive self-supervised learning across the RMIS benchmark (19 datasets across 4 modalities), the 28M-parameter FISHER achieves state-of-the-art anomaly detection and fault classification while being up to $16\times$ more parameter-efficient than monolithic vision-language adaptations.

    \item \emph{PowerPM: Foundation Model for Power Systems (Tu et al., NeurIPS 2024)}~\cite{tu2024powerpm}: Presented at NeurIPS 2024, Tu et al. formulate PowerPM, a 115M-parameter foundation model specifically engineered for electricity time series (ETS). Electricity telemetry possesses strict topological hierarchy (city $\to$ district $\to$ user) and strong meteorological covariate dependencies. PowerPM unifies a Temporal Transformer with a Relational Graph Convolutional Network (R-GCN) over power grid topologies. Pretrained on massive private and public grid datasets via Dual-View Contrastive Learning (DVCL) and masked ETS sequence prediction:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{PowerPM}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Masked}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{DVCL}}(\mathbf{Z}_{\text{grid}}, \mathbf{Z}_{\text{temp}}),
    \end{equation}
    PowerPM demonstrates exceptional transferability across downstream load forecasting, grid stability assessment, and consumer behavior profiling.

    \item \emph{FTP-1: Generalist Foundation Tactile Policy (Yuan et al., 2026)}~\cite{yuan2026ftp1}: Addressing the hardware lock-in of robotic tactile perception, Yuan et al. establish FTP-1 (180M parameters), the first sensor-agnostic generalist tactile policy. Tactile streams exhibit drastic cross-hardware variation, spanning optical-tactile gel cameras (DIGIT, GelSight), piezoresistive sensor arrays, and barometric contact skins. FTP-1 maps these disparate physical streams into \emph{morphology-aware latent unified tokens} conditioned on physical geometry descriptors. Pretrained across 3,000 hours of contact manipulation data spanning 21 distinct sensors and human-robot demonstrations, FTP-1 achieves a $+17.2\%$ success gain on known sensors and a $+31\%$ zero-shot success gain when transferred to entirely unseen tactile sensors in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

    \item \emph{TouchWorld: Hierarchical Predictive and Reactive Tactile World Model (Zhou et al., 2026)}~\cite{zhou2026touchworld}: Zhou et al. formulate TouchWorld (350M parameters), uniting anticipatory world modeling with high-frequency reactive control. In dexterous manipulation, slip and tactile hysteresis occur within milliseconds. TouchWorld decouples manipulation into a low-frequency predictive world model that autoregressively forecasts spatio-temporal contact patch trajectories $\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{t+1:t+H}$ from multimodal visual-tactile contexts, and a high-frequency ($>100\,\text{Hz}$) reactive policy that executes localized closed-loop residual corrections over the EgoTouch bimanual contact corpus.

    \item \emph{TS-JEPA: Temporal Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (Ennadir et al., NeurIPS 2024 Workshop)}~\cite{ennadir2025tsjepa}: Ennadir et al. extend LeCun's Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture to time series. Conventional autoregressive and masked autoencoding models optimize point-wise reconstruction losses in the observation space, rendering them excessively sensitive to irreducible sensor noise and high-frequency stochastic fluctuations. TS-JEPA discards observation reconstruction entirely: it maps context and target windows into an abstract latent metric space, training a latent energy predictor to minimize:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{TS-JEPA}} = \left\| \mathbf{s}_{\text{target}} - g_\phi(\mathbf{s}_{\text{context}}, \mathbf{z}) \right\|_2^2,
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{z}$ is a temporal mask conditioning token. By enforcing representation invariance in latent space, TS-JEPA yields superior linear probing and zero-shot transfer for classification and long-horizon forecasting across UCR/UEA and Monash benchmarks.

    \item \emph{RATFM: Retrieval-Augmented Foundation Model for Anomaly Detection (Maru and Sato, 2025)}~\cite{maru2025ratfm}: Maru and Sato address the inability of standard zero-shot TSFMs to condition on normal operational prototypes without parameter fine-tuning. RATFM (110M parameters) equips foundation transformers with a non-parametric test-time retrieval memory. Given an evaluation window, RATFM queries an in-domain target datastore to retrieve top-$k$ normal subsequence exemplars, feeding them as in-context adaptation anchors to compute reconstruction discrepancy scores. On the multi-domain UCR Anomaly Archive (spanning 9 physical domains), RATFM matches the performance of in-domain supervised fine-tuning while requiring zero parameter modifications.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unified Post-Training Paradigms: Parameter-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation and Multimodal Generative Foundations}
\label{sec:posttraining_petsa_aurora}

As time series foundation models transition from static pretraining on diverse corpora to downstream operational serving, the critical role of post-training has emerged as a distinct, formal sub-discipline. Pretraining alone cannot guarantee optimal performance under downstream domain shifts, specialized task topologies, and hard inference latency envelopes. Recent 2025--2026 breakthroughs establish systematic post-training taxonomies, parameter-efficient test-time adaptation (TTA), and multimodal generative foundation architectures:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{A Unifying Framework for Post-Training in TSFMs (Xie et al., 2026)}~\cite{xie2026posttraining}: Xie et al. establish the first comprehensive taxonomical foundation for post-training in time series foundation models. They argue that while pretraining endows models with broad cross-domain representations, downstream deployment inevitably encounters severe domain shift, task heterogeneity, label scarcity, and strict edge latency constraints. Xie et al. organize post-training interventions into five foundational pillars categorized by their operational locus in the inference pipeline: (1)~\emph{Parameter Adaptation} (white-box fine-tuning, dynamic low-rank adapters, and prefix tuning), (2)~\emph{Context Augmentation} (in-context retrieval, prompt engineering, and multiscale patching), (3)~\emph{Model Composition} (cascaded architectures, foundation-specialist routing, and hierarchical ensembling), (4)~\emph{Output Processing \& Uncertainty Control} (distribution-free conformal calibration, residual shrinkage, and quantile reconciliation), and (5)~\emph{Compression \& Specialization} (low-bit quantization, knowledge distillation, and hardware-aware pruning). They formalize downstream post-training as minimizing expected risk under unknown target distribution shift:
    \begin{equation}
        \min_{\theta_{\text{post}}, \mathcal{A}_{\text{ctx}}, \mathcal{C}_{\text{out}}} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}} \left[ \ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{\text{out}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\theta_{\text{post}}}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{ctx}}(\mathbf{x}))\right), \mathbf{y}\right) \right] + \Omega(\theta_{\text{post}} - \theta_{\text{pre}}),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{ctx}}$ denotes context transformation, $\theta_{\text{post}}$ parameter adaptation regularized by distance metric $\Omega$, and $\mathcal{C}_{\text{out}}$ calibrated output processing, providing an overarching conceptual map that synthesizes post-training across research groups.

    \item \emph{PETSA: Parameter-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation (Medeiros et al., ICML 2025)}~\cite{medeiros2025petsa}: Presented at the ICML 2025 Workshop on Test-Time Adaptation, Medeiros et al. resolve the prohibitive compute and memory costs of full-model test-time updates. Real-world temporal data frequently undergo non-stationary regime shifts during online inference. Rather than updating billions of foundation parameters via gradient backpropagation, PETSA (Parameter-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation) introduces lightweight low-rank adapters ($<0.2\text{M}$ tunable parameters, $<1\%$ of baseline parameters) coupled with dynamic feature-level gating across projection layers. To prevent catastrophic overfitting on short streaming windows, PETSA introduces a unified multi-objective test-time objective:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{PETSA}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Huber}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t, \mathbf{y}_t) + \lambda_f \mathcal{L}_{\text{freq}}(\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_t), \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{y}_t)) + \lambda_p \mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}}(\hat{\mathbf{Z}}_t, \mathbf{Z}_t),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Huber}}$ suppresses heavy-tailed sensor outliers, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{freq}}$ matches spectral power distributions via the Fast Fourier Transform $\mathcal{F}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}}$ preserves latent structural invariants across consecutive temporal patches $\mathbf{Z}_t$. Across non-stationary ETT, Weather, and Electricity benchmarks, PETSA achieves up to $31.8\%$ MSE reductions while accelerating test-time adaptation runtime by $4.8\times$ compared to standard full-model TTA.

    \item \emph{Aurora: Universal Generative Multimodal Foundation Model (Wu et al., ICLR 2026)}~\cite{wu2026aurora}: Published at ICLR 2026, Wu et al. tackle the fundamental ambiguity of unimodal time series forecasting, where identical historical numerical sequences can evolve into drastically divergent future trajectories due to latent domain conditions. Aurora introduces a universal generative multimodal foundation model pretrained on the Cross-Domain Multimodal Time Series Corpus (CD-MTSC). Aurora features a Cross-Modality Encoder that executes textual token distillation and bidirectional cross-attention: rich natural-language contextual descriptors (e.g., weather metadata, macroeconomic commentary, operational state logs) directly modulate the patch embeddings of the temporal encoder. Furthermore, Aurora replaces deterministic point prediction heads with a generative diffusion head that optimizes continuous score matching:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{diff}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t_d, \mathbf{y}_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}} \left[ \left\| \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta\left(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t_d}} \mathbf{y}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t_d}} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}; t_d, \mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ts}}, \mathbf{c}_{\text{text}})\right) \right\|_2^2 \right],
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ts}}, \mathbf{c}_{\text{text}})$ is the joint multimodal embedding and $t_d$ is the diffusion timestep. Aurora achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot multimodal generative forecasting, outperforming both specialized unimodal TSFMs and fine-tuned LLM backbones across 12 diverse cross-domain benchmarks.
\end{itemize}
