\section{Evaluations, Benchmarks, and Critical Perspectives}
\label{sec:eval}

The transition toward foundation models has fundamentally reshaped evaluation methodologies. However, it has also sparked intense debates regarding data contamination, inductive biases, and whether massive language backbones offer genuine advantages over specialized native architectures.

\subsection{Standardized Foundation Benchmarks}

Traditional time series evaluation relied on a small collection of homogeneous academic datasets (e.g., ETT, Weather, Electricity, Traffic). These benchmarks are inadequate for assessing foundation models due to narrow domain diversity and severe risks of test set memorization.

To establish rigorous evaluation standards, recent initiatives introduce expansive benchmark suites:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{GIFT-Eval}~\cite{salesforce2024gifteval}: Introduced by Salesforce Research, GIFT-Eval comprises over $143{,}000$ distinct time series spanning 7 diverse domains, 10 sampling frequencies, and both univariate and multivariate regimes. It establishes standardized metrics (CRPS, MASE, WAPE) across zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuned settings.
    \item \emph{fev-bench}~\cite{shchur2025fevbench}: Introduced by Shchur et al. (AWS and AutoGluon), fev-bench formulates a realistic benchmark encompassing 100 forecasting tasks across 7 domains. Crucially, 46 of these tasks incorporate dynamic covariates (e.g., promotional flags, weather drivers, calendar events) evaluated via the open-source \texttt{fev} library using bootstrapped confidence intervals and win-rate skill scores, directly addressing the historic deficiency where foundation models underperformed simple baselines by omitting exogenous features.
    \item \emph{It's TIME}~\cite{qiao2026time}: Qiao et al. (joint work between Tsinghua THUML and Ming Jin's group) establish next-generation benchmarks addressing multi-granularity task evaluation and rigorous train-test contamination audits, establishing standardized leakage-free splits across diverse operational horizons.
    \item \emph{Beyond Numerical Time Series}~\cite{chen2026beyondnumerical}: Chen et al. establish a unified multimodal evaluation framework pairing continuous quantitative sensors with heterogeneous textual descriptions, news contexts, and structural calendar metadata.
    \item \emph{AION}~\cite{zhan2026aion}: Zhan, Jin et al. formulate next-generation benchmarks moving beyond static univariate forecasting to evaluate agentic tool-use, multi-step structured reasoning, and contextual decision support over temporal observations.
    \item \emph{TimesX}~\cite{liu2026timesx}: Published at ICML 2026 by researchers from Georgia Tech and Google, TimesX provides a standardized benchmark for context-enriched multimodal forecasting, pairing high-frequency sensors with text narratives, calendar events, and exogenous market drivers across diverse global domains.
    \item \emph{TimeSeriesExam}~\cite{cai2024timeseriesexam}: Developed by the CMU MOMENT team, TimeSeriesExam formulates a diagnostic capability examination for foundation models. Rather than evaluating generic mean-squared errors on arbitrary test splits, it probes specific foundational abilities: trend extrapolation, seasonality decomposition, phase-shift invariance, and anomaly localization.
    \item \emph{LiveHouse-TS}~\cite{wen2026livehouse}: An open-world living benchmark platform that streams fresh real-world telemetry in real time, preventing static benchmark memorization and providing continuous evaluation under non-stationary concept drift.
    \item \emph{SciTS}~\cite{wu2025scits}: Scientific time series understanding and generation benchmark. Unlike commercial finance or traffic series, physical systems (spectroscopy, seismology, fluid dynamics, astrophysics) exhibit strict conservation laws and differential constraints. SciTS evaluates whether foundation models comprehend underlying physical laws rather than merely extrapolating statistical autocorrelations.
    \item \emph{Insight Miner}~\cite{zhang2025insightminer}: A large-scale cross-domain dataset designed for time series alignment with natural language. Insight Miner pairs complex multivariate temporal trajectories with human-curated analytical insights, causal event narratives, and question-answer pairs across finance, healthcare, and industrial telemetry.
    \item \emph{Time-MMD}~\cite{yue2024timemmd}: A multi-domain multimodal dataset evaluating the integration of numerical time series with unstructured text reports, news articles, and domain metadata across 9 industries.
    \item \emph{Monash Time Series Repository}: The classical zero-shot transfer benchmark comprising dozens of public collections across economics, energy, and meteorology, widely adopted to evaluate out-of-distribution generalization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Critical Perspectives: Are LLMs Actually Useful for Time Series?}

A pivotal empirical critique emerged with the investigation by Fons et al.~\cite{fons2024arellmsuseful}, entitled \emph{``Are Language Models Actually Useful for Time Series Forecasting?''}
Through extensive ablation experiments across multiple LLM-based forecasters (including GPT4TS~\cite{zhou2023onefitsall} and Time-LLM~\cite{jin2023timellm}), the authors isolated the impact of pre-trained language weights by comparing:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Frozen pre-trained language weights (e.g., GPT-2, Llama-2);
    \item Randomly initialized transformer weights with the same architecture;
    \item Compact specialized baselines (such as PatchTST~\cite{nie2023patchtst} and lightweight MLPs).
\end{enumerate}

Their empirical findings revealed striking conclusions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Replacing pre-trained language weights with \emph{randomly initialized weights} resulted in comparable, and in several cases superior, forecasting accuracy.
    \item Much of the empirical gains reported in early LLM4TS literature originated from the structural benefits of \emph{subseries patching} and normalization, rather than any semantic or temporal reasoning inherent in linguistic pre-training.
    \item In terms of operational efficiency, frozen $7\text{B}$ parameter language models require up to $100\times$ more memory and $50\times$ longer inference latency than compact native models like TTM~\cite{ekambaram2024ttm} ($8\text{M}$ parameters) or Timer~\cite{liu2024timer} ($84\text{M}$ parameters), which consistently achieve competitive or superior accuracy.
\end{itemize}

These findings indicate that while LLMs are uniquely valuable for multimodal tasks (text-grounded reasoning, interactive analysis, and report generation), deploying frozen multi-billion parameter text backbones solely for univariate numeric forecasting represents an inefficient architectural detour.

Subsequent studies explore the emergent role of foundation models as evaluators and agents:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{TimeVista}~\cite{chen2026timevista}: Chen et al. (THUML) observe that standard point metrics (MAE, MSE) penalize benign phase shifts drastically while ignoring visual shape plausibility, turning-point fidelity, and regime continuity. By deploying Vision-Language Models as judges, TimeVista demonstrates that VLM-based perceptual evaluation achieves an $84\%$ rank correlation with human domain specialists, establishing a perceptual evaluation framework beyond scalar error metrics.
    \item \emph{Forecast Workflow Bench}~\cite{nagashima2026forecastworkflow}: Nagashima investigates whether LLM agents can orchestrate time-series foundation models under realistic operational tool budgets. Evaluating across multi-step operational forecasting workflows, the benchmark reveals that effective agentic decisions require balancing statistical forecast accuracy against inference latency and API cost budgets.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Contamination, Temporal Leakage, and Probabilistic Reliability}

\subsubsection{Benchmark Contamination Audits}
A critical vulnerability in contemporary foundation model evaluation is \emph{benchmark contamination}. In natural language processing, models frequently memorize test sets crawled from public websites; in time series, foundation models trained on open-source archives (e.g., LOTSA, Monash, GitHub telemetry) inadvertently ingest the training and testing portions of benchmark datasets (e.g., ETT, Weather)~\cite{gong2025rethinkingeval}.

Gong et al.~\cite{gong2025rethinkingeval} systematically audited 12 major foundation models and discovered widespread temporal overlap between pretraining datasets and standard test benchmarks. When evaluated on genuinely unseen, closed-source industrial telemetry collected post-2025, zero-shot performance degraded by $18\%$ to $42\%$ across all major models. This underscores the urgent need for living, dynamic benchmarks (such as LiveHouse-TS~\cite{wen2026livehouse}) where evaluation datasets are continuously refreshed.

\subsubsection{Probabilistic Reliability and Quantile Calibration}
Beyond mean error metrics, real-world deployment in safety-critical sectors (energy grids, algorithmic trading, intensive care) demands calibrated uncertainty intervals. In a recent rigorous audit, Li et al.~\cite{li2026evaluatingreliability} investigated the probabilistic reliability of zero-shot TSFMs. Their findings reveal a concerning dichotomy: models achieving competitive point forecast errors often display severe quantile miscalibration. Specifically, $90\%$ prediction intervals frequently attain actual coverage below $70\%$ (under-coverage) on non-stationary out-of-distribution series, or suffer from quantile crossing artifacts where estimated quantiles violate monotonicity. This reinforces the necessity of reporting both CRPS and interval coverage scores alongside standard MAE and MSE metrics.

\subsection{Empirical Scaling Laws and Meta-Regression in Temporal Data}
\label{sec:scaling_laws}

Do foundation models for time series exhibit the predictable power-law scaling observed in NLP?
Recent empirical studies on large pre-trained models—notably Time-MoE~\cite{shi2024timemoe} ($2.4\text{B}$ parameters, $300\text{B}$ points), Sundial~\cite{thuml2025sundial} ($1.5\text{B}$ parameters), Timer-S1~\cite{thuml2026timers1} ($8.3\text{B}$ parameters), and Toto 2.0~\cite{datadog2026toto2} ($2.5\text{B}$ parameters)—demonstrate that when data quality and volume are rigorously maintained, validation loss follows a bivariate power law as a joint function of non-embedding parameter count $N$ and unique training tokens $D$:
\begin{equation}
    L(N, D) = \left( \frac{N_c}{N} \right)^{\alpha_N} + \left( \frac{D_c}{D} \right)^{\alpha_D} + L_0,
\end{equation}
where $N_c, D_c$ denote characteristic scale constants and $L_0$ represents the irreducible entropy of stochastic observation noise. Given the training compute budget $C \approx 6 N D$, the optimal parameter allocation follows $N_{\text{opt}} \propto C^{\frac{\alpha_D}{\alpha_N + \alpha_D}}$ and $D_{\text{opt}} \propto C^{\frac{\alpha_N}{\alpha_N + \alpha_D}}$.

Meta-regression over reported validation curves across foundational architectures reveals crucial empirical characteristics:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Fitted Exponents}: Time-MoE~\cite{shi2024timemoe} reports $\alpha_N \approx 0.089$ and $\alpha_D \approx 0.112$ across models ranging from $50\text{M}$ to $2.4\text{B}$ parameters on Time-300B; Sundial~\cite{thuml2025sundial} reports $\alpha_N \approx 0.076$ on UTSD-3; and Toto 2.0~\cite{datadog2026toto2} reports $\alpha_N \approx 0.081$ and $\alpha_D \approx 0.098$ under Maximal Update Parametrization ($\mu\text{P}$). In comparison, LLM language pretraining exhibits $\alpha_N \approx 0.076$--$0.088$ and $\alpha_D \approx 0.095$--$0.120$. This confirms that temporal transformers obey bona fide neural scaling laws.
    \item \emph{The Temporal Entropy Saturation Barrier}: In time series, $\alpha_N$ exhibits early saturation when scaling beyond $1\text{B}$ parameters if trained solely on public sensor repositories. Because real-world telemetry frequently contains repetitive stationary waveforms with low Kolmogorov complexity, models quickly exhaust novel temporal structures. To sustain power-law scaling ($\alpha_N > 0$), state-of-the-art pipelines (Chronos, Time-MoE, Tabby~\cite{xie2026tabby}) inject synthetic generative processes (mixtures of non-linear differential equations and Gaussian process kernels), proving that synthetic diversity is mandatory to maintain compute-optimal scaling in time series.
\end{itemize}

Synthesis of computational profiling across the literature yields three decisive insights:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{The Autoregressive Generation Bottleneck}: Models relying on scalar or token-wise autoregression (e.g., original Chronos~\cite{ansari2024chronos} iterating over 4096 bins) incur $H$ sequential attention forward passes to forecast an $H$-step horizon. In production benchmarks, Chronos-Bolt resolves this bottleneck by shifting from point-wise autoregression to patch-level direct quantile regression, achieving a documented $250\times$ inference speedup and $20\times$ memory reduction while matching or exceeding zero-shot accuracy.
    \item \emph{The Extreme Throughput Gap (Mixers vs. Reprogrammed LLMs)}: Lightweight architectural designs—exemplified by IBM's Tiny Time Mixers (TTM~\cite{ekambaram2024ttm}, 1M--8M parameters) and LightGTS~\cite{wang2025lightgts} (5.8M parameters)—execute full batch inference in under $5\text{ms}$ on commodity hardware with zero KV-cache memory overhead. In contrast, reprogrammed LLMs (Time-LLM~\cite{jin2023timellm}, AutoTimes~\cite{liu2024autotimes}) require multi-second latency per inference batch and allocate over $14\text{GB}$ of GPU VRAM. For high-frequency industrial automation where millisecond response times are mandatory, compact native mixers represent the only viable foundation paradigm.
    \item \emph{In-Context Bayesian Inference vs. Score-Based Diffusion}: Prior-Data Fitted Networks (TabPFN-TS~\cite{muller2025tabpfnts}) offer unique operational trade-offs: while context-sequence attention scales quadratically with history length, they execute zero gradient updates, eliminating all fine-tuning optimizer overhead. Conversely, continuous score-based flow matching (FlowState~\cite{king2025flowstate}, UrbanDiT~\cite{li2024urbandit}) demands multiple numerical ODE integration steps ($K \in [10, 50]$), exchanging inference latency for mathematically continuous, sampling-rate-equivariant trajectories.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{HoliBench: Holistic Cross-Platform Profiling}: Chakrabarti et al.~\cite{chakrabarti2026holibench} introduce HoliBench, a standardized benchmarking and deployment toolkit that jointly characterizes accuracy, latency, and energy across seven device classes, eight inference backends, and three quantization levels (FP16, INT8, INT4). HoliBench uncovers that integer post-training quantization (PTQ) reduces latency only on architectures equipped with specialized low-precision compute pipelines (e.g., INT8 Tensor Cores or NEON vector units); on general-purpose microcontrollers lacking integer SIMD, quantization merely reduces memory footprint while potentially increasing execution latency due to dequantization overhead. Furthermore, HoliBench demonstrates that standalone single-model profiles compose with high fidelity ($1.2\%$ latency and $2.5\%$ power error) under co-resident execution, enabling optimal multi-model pipeline configuration without combinatorial profiling.
    \item \emph{Resource-Aware Mixed-Precision FPGA Quantization}: Ling et al.~\cite{ling2024resource} resolve the hardware-level bottleneck of deploying time-series Transformers onto resource-constrained embedded FPGAs (Xilinx Spartan-7 XC7S15). By formulating a resource-aware mixed-precision quantization methodology (allocating INT8 to sensitive cross-attention maps and INT4 to feed-forward blocks) and optimizing block RAM (BRAM) storage templates, they achieve hardware resource estimation within $3\%$ of physical silicon synthesis, unlocking deployability for previously non-synthesizable transformer configurations.
    \item \emph{Carbon-Aware Dynamic Dispatch (FM-CAC)}: Yang et al.~\cite{yang2026fmcac} formulate FM-CAC, establishing a proactive carbon-aware control framework for battery-buffered edge AI systems. By deploying edge-friendly zero-shot TSFMs to forecast temporal marginal emissions and grid carbon intensity, FM-CAC feeds predictions into a dynamic programming solver with deferred cost attribution. This decouples energy harvesting from compute execution, reducing carbon emissions by up to $65.6\%$ while maintaining near-maximum inference accuracy across continuous edge vision and time-series pipelines.
\end{enumerate}

As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:energy_quantization_benchmarks}, lightweight architectures (e.g., Cheraghinia MLP~\cite{cheraghinia2025lightweight}, TTM~\cite{ekambaram2024ttm}, and APEX-Edge~\cite{pradhan2026apex}) consume between $0.18\text{ mJ}$ and $28.5\text{ mJ}$ per inference, operating comfortably within battery-buffered and energy-harvesting IoT regimes with negligible ($<1.2\%$) degradation under INT8 quantization. In contrast, multi-billion parameter LLMs demand over $18\text{ Joules}$ per forward pass and over $14\text{ GB}$ of VRAM, underscoring that compact, quantization-resilient native models are mandatory for sustainable edge intelligence.

Synthesis of these evaluative frontiers yields four critical insights:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Causal Audits and the Persistence Bias Failure Mode}: Jander, van Heeswijk, and Mes~\cite{jander2026causal} demonstrate that transitioning from bespoke models to foundation forecasters creates systemic concentration risk. By probing state-of-the-art foundation models (Chronos-2~\cite{ansari2025chronos2}, TimesFM-2.5~\cite{das2024timesfm}, MOIRAI~\cite{woo2024moirai}) using synthetic interventional data generators, their causal audit reveals that foundation models exhibit a severe \emph{persistence bias}. Specifically, when probed with processes exhibiting delayed interventional responses, sudden regime shifts, or unobserved confounders, models default to extrapolating local lag trends, severely underestimating the magnitude and velocity of structural inflections.
    \item \emph{Realistic Benchmarking for Temporal Causal Discovery}: Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2023causaltime} establish CausalTime (NeurIPS 2023), resolving a core bottleneck in temporal causal validation. Prior benchmarks relied on oversimplified linear autoregressive models with Gaussian noise, producing synthetic evaluations disconnected from real-world physics. CausalTime utilizes deep invertible generative neural networks and normalizing flows to capture complex nonlinear dynamics and heteroskedasticity while deriving exact ground-truth causal DAGs, creating a rigorous standard benchmark for evaluating causal graph discovery.
    \item \emph{Principled Test-Time Adaptation Benchmarking (FAC)}: Wang et al.~\cite{wang2026principled} critique existing TTA evaluations for subtle future-information leakage and ad-hoc batch scheduling. They establish a standardized leak-free streaming benchmark suite and introduce Frequency-Aware Calibration (FAC). FAC proves that adapting models directly in the raw temporal domain frequently amplifies high-frequency measurement noise into destructive gradient shocks; conversely, isolating adapter updates to orthogonal low- and mid-frequency Fourier sub-bands preserves base model stability while yielding consistent accuracy improvements across non-stationary streaming horizons.
    \item \emph{Living Leaderboard Ecosystems (The TIME Benchmark)}: Addressing the rapid obsolescence of static benchmark datasets, Qiao et al.~\cite{qiao2026time} introduce the TIME benchmark (50 fresh datasets, 98 tasks) and the public Hugging Face TIME-Leaderboard (\url{https://huggingface.co/spaces/Real-TSF/TIME-leaderboard}). By pairing automated weekly evaluation pipelines with pattern-level time series decomposition, TIME evaluates foundation models across multi-scale periodicity, non-stationarity, and noise regimes, establishing a living, contamination-resistant community reference standard for zero-shot time-series intelligence.
\end{enumerate}

These living and agentic evaluation frameworks illuminate two fundamental paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Continuous Live Evaluation vs. Static Overfitting}: Garza et al.~\cite{garza2026impermanent} instantiate \emph{Impermanent} on top-400 GitHub activity streams. Because software repositories experience organic shocks (major version releases, viral social adoption, tooling migrations), they exhibit intense non-stationarity without synthetic artifacts. Crucially, Impermanent scores forecasts sequentially over time on live data streams with daily leaderboard updates (\url{https://impermanent.timecopilot.dev}). Their findings expose that zero-shot foundation models showing near-optimal static error rates frequently deteriorate when evaluated under sustained streaming evolution, underscoring that static benchmarks reward memorization and lookahead leakage rather than true temporal generalizability.
    \item \emph{Multi-Turn Dialogue and Decision Gaps}: Kong et al.~\cite{kong2026timesagemt} construct \emph{TimeSage-MT}, bridging the gap between single-step predictions and practical decision-making workflows. Across 2,680 dialogue turns in eight domains (finance, energy, transportation, healthcare, meteorology, manufacturing, retail, and server telemetry), they discover that even frontier closed-source LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) suffer sharp performance drops exceeding $35\%$ when progressing from exploratory questions to complex multi-step decision tasks. These failures stem primarily from three bottlenecks: loss of intermediate memory over long dialogue horizons, inability to calibrate forecast uncertainty across tool invocations, and domain hallucination when resolving conflicting statistical evidence.
\end{enumerate}
